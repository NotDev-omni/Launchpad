What we changed and why

Direction 02 · marketplace
01 · Risk is a column

Trust sits in the table

OpenSea shows floor, volume, owners. We add Risk and LP as first-class sortable columns, right next to price. If trust is the thing you're selling, it cannot live behind a tab — it has to be scannable in the same glance as the number people came for.

02 · Rarity is the palette

Color is never decoration

OpenSea's cards are neutral chrome around artwork. Ours are tier-coded on the border, badge, and base rail, so you read the rarity distribution of an entire grid without looking at a single trait. It's the one saturated colour in the product.

03 · The lane switch

One toggle, two products

OpenSea ships a Collector/Pro toggle. Ours goes further — flipping to Raise swaps the accent from hot to cool, kills the live ticker, drops density, and re-labels the trust columns to audit and escrow. Same engine, deliberately different room. Try it in the header.

04 · Hard edges

Notched panels, not rounded cards

Every marketplace in crypto uses the same 12px rounded card. The clipped corner is our structural signature — it reads as hardware and industrial, matches the crate mechanic, and makes a screenshot recognisable as ours from across a feed.

05 · Sweep is sticky

The bulk action follows you

Sweeping is how volume actually happens, so the sweep bar is pinned to the viewport instead of buried above the grid. Drag it and the total updates live against real floor prices.

06 · Typography does the work

Oxanium + IBM Plex

Angular display face for names and numbers that should feel like a HUD; Plex Sans and Mono for everything that must stay readable at 12px. Every figure is tabular, so columns line up and scanning down a price column is effortless.
