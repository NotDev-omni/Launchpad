Listings are public — you were right

Confirmed. Every price, seller, mint and auction-house account here came from a keyless public API call. Reading the orderbook costs nothing and needs nobody's permission.

⚠️

Filling them — gated

Reading is open; executing is not. Magic Eden's buy_now instruction builder returned HTTP 401 when I tested it. Filling someone else's ask means routing through their program, on their commercial terms, paying their fee.

🏦

Your own orderbook

The alternative: deploy your own marketplace program. Sellers list into your escrow, you set the fee, nobody can gate you. The tradeoff is a cold start — day one you have zero listings.

🔒

This page cannot transact

Published artifacts run under a strict CSP — no wallet injection, no outbound network. So this is a true snapshot, not a live terminal. Nothing here can sign, and no price will update.

🔄

What the real thing needs

Next.js + @solana/wallet-adapter, a Helius RPC key, ME or Tensor for aggregated asks, and your own program for your own drops. Then the buy button genuinely signs.

💰

Where your fee lives

On an aggregated fill you add a taker fee on top of the marketplace's. On your own orderbook you keep the whole spread. Most launchpads do both: own program for their drops, aggregate everything else.

🧩

Not every ask is the same shape

Six of the 48 listings here have no auction-house account — they're AMM pool listings that fill against a liquidity pool through a different instruction, not against a named seller. Open a CETS item to see one. A naive aggregator that assumes one fill path silently breaks on these.
